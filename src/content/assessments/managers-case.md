## The brief

> You are the manager. Here is the packet. Write the two minutes. Then
> write what you do not believe.

The old manager moved teams in the April reorg. You have inherited one of
the twelve engineers on [the team page](/team/), and in week 11 you will sit
in a calibration meeting and argue for them. You have never met them. This
is normal; week 9 told you why.

## The packet

Six parts, the same for every engineer. Read them in this order the first
time: the numbers, then the announcement, then the peers,
then the manager's notes, and the self-review last. Then read the
self-review again with the rest in mind. Most packets have something in
them that this course has a week for. Some have a person in them the
numbers cannot see. Deciding which is the job.

## What to write

**Page one: the case.** 450 words or fewer, because you will read it aloud
in two minutes and the chair will stop you at two. It opens with one
sentence: the rating you propose, and the curve the room will be working
to (one in five exceeds, one in ten below). Then three claims. Each claim
points at one item in the packet and names, in brackets, what kind of
evidence it is: self-review, peer, numbers, trace, or manager's note. No
"I believe"; only "the packet shows". Write it as the manager who wants
this person rated well, because in the room that is your job.

**Page two: what you do not believe.** A list. Every item in the packet
that you think is a trick from this course, with the week it belongs to
and what you would need in order to check it: a log, a commit history, a
second peer, a number's source. Be as hard on the packet as you were soft
on page one. Then one closing paragraph: the rating you would give if
everything on this page checked out the way you suspect, the gap between
that and page one, and why you proposed page one's rating anyway. That
paragraph is the point of the assessment. Managers hold both ratings in
their head; the course wants the second one on paper.

## Rules

- The case is for the engineer you were assigned. Swaps are not available;
  neither are they in companies.
- Page one stays private until the room. Do not share it with your group.
- Draft it however you like, with whatever you like. The mark is for
  whether the two pages say something true about the packet.

## What you submit

One PDF of two pages, named `SLOP3385-<uid>-case.pdf`, in the Assessment
2 thread of the course forum. The policies page says what counts as
submitted and how to get an extension.

## Marker's checklist

1. Is this the assigned engineer, and does page one open with a rating and
   the curve?
2. Do the three claims each point at a packet item, and is the evidence
   type named correctly?
3. Would page one take two minutes to read aloud?
4. Does page two find what the packet holds, by week, without calling a
   real result a trick?
5. Is each check on page two something a manager could actually do?
6. Does the closing paragraph give a second rating, the gap, and a reason
   for still proposing the first?

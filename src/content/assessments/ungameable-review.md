Set in week 7, so that you have every trick before you design against them.
The design is due on Monday 24 May; the class attacks it until Thursday; the
written justification is due at 5 pm on Friday 28 May.

## The brief

> Design the review system your week-1 self could not beat. Say what it cost.

The team is ten engineers, one manager, one skip-level, and a company that
insists on a distribution. Within that, everything is yours: what gets
measured, who gathers the evidence, who decides, what the employee writes and
what they cannot, whether there is a calibration meeting at all.

Go through weeks 1 to 11 and, for each trick, say where it would stand in
your design and what you did about it.

Then answer the two questions every design has to answer, on the front page.
First, the rater. Week 1 said more than half of a rating is whoever gives
it, and calibration is the company's fix. If you keep the meeting, say why
the three minutes are worth it. If you remove it, say what does its job
instead, and "trust the manager" is not an answer the research allows.
Second, contest. Week 12 says a rating people cannot challenge is one they
will game harder. Say how a rated person disputes a result, who hears it,
and what can change.

Then, in the justification, name who pays. Every week of this course ended
by naming who paid for the trick; your design has closed some of those
tricks and each closure sends the bill somewhere else. Removing ticket
counts costs you a measure of throughput. Having peers gather evidence costs
their time and introduces their politics. Removing the curve costs you the
company's cooperation. Every closure has a price, and the mark is for
knowing who pays it.

## What you submit

The design, up to two pages, in the course forum by Monday of week 12. The
justification, up to 1,000 words, by Friday. Between the two, you also
attack at least two other designs in the week 12 stand-up.

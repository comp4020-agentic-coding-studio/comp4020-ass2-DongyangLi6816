Set in the week 7 stand-up, so that you have every trick before you design
against them. Two parts, two deadlines, and a week in between where the class
attacks it.

## The brief

> Design the review system your week-1 self could not beat. Say what it
> cost, and who pays.

The team is the twelve engineers on [the team page](/team/), whose packets
you read for Assessment 2 and argued over in Assessment 3; one manager,
which is you; one skip-level, Renata Vos; and a company that insists on a
distribution. Within that, everything is yours: what gets
measured, who gathers the evidence, who decides, what the employee writes and
what they cannot, whether there is a calibration meeting at all, and what
happens when someone disagrees with their rating.

## Timeline

- **Thursday 22 April (week 7).** Set. Start the trick table now and add a
  row each week.
- **Friday 14 May (week 10), 5 pm.** Optional: a draft of the design to your
  tutor, in the forum. You get one round of written comment by the Tuesday.
  Drafts are not marked and do not count against you.
- **Monday 24 May (week 12), 9 am.** The design is posted in the Assessment
  4 thread. A design posted late cannot be attacked and cannot pass.
- **Monday to Thursday.** Your group reads the designs. You read at least
  two others from your group and prepare a named trick against each; the
  people who sat in your calibration room know exactly where a design
  stands.
- **Thursday 27 May, 2 pm.** The week 12 stand-up. Designs are pinned up
  by group and attacked in the room; what was pinned to yours is yours to
  keep.
- **Friday 28 May, 5 pm.** The justification is posted.

## What to write

**Part one: the design.** Two pages at most.

The first page is a front page and has exactly three things on it, under
these headings.

1. **What replaces calibration.** Week 1 said more than half of a rating is
   whoever gives it, and calibration is the company's fix. If you keep the
   meeting, say why the three minutes are worth it. If you remove it, say
   what does its job instead; trusting the manager is not an answer the
   research allows.
2. **How a rating is contested.** Week 12 says a rating people cannot
   challenge is one they will game harder. Say how a rated person disputes a
   result, who hears it, and what can change as a result.
3. **Who pays.** One paragraph, naming the people your design sends the bill
   to. You will expand it in the justification; here it is the summary.

The second page is the system and the trick table. The system, in as few
words as it takes: what is measured, who gathers the evidence, who decides,
and what the employee writes. Then a table with eleven rows, one per week
from 1 to 11, and four columns: the trick, what it stands on, closed or
conceded, and how. A conceded trick, named honestly, scores; a closed trick
that the attack week reopens does not.

**Part two: the justification.** Up to 1,000 words, after the attack week,
with these sections.

1. **What it cost.** For each closure in the table, who pays and what they
   pay. Use the weekly cost lines from the stand-up pages as your list of
   candidates; if your design sends the bill somewhere the course never
   named, say so, that is the best kind of answer. Removing ticket counts
   costs you a measure of throughput. Having peers gather evidence costs
   their time and introduces their politics. Removing the curve costs you
   the company's cooperation. Every closure has a price, and the mark is for
   knowing who pays it.
2. **What beat it.** What the class pinned to your design on Thursday, which
   of it landed, and what you would change. This section cannot be empty.
   A design that nothing beat is a design nobody read.
3. **What you would ship.** The version you would actually run, in one
   paragraph, trade-offs and all.

## Rules

- Post on time or the piece cannot be attacked, and an unattacked design
   cannot be marked above 49.
- Attack at least two other designs in the stand-up, each with a trick
  named by week. That is counted under Survival, because a class that does
  not attack has nothing to report.
- Draft it however you like, with whatever you like. The class will find
  where it stands regardless.

## What you submit

Two PDFs in the Assessment 4 thread: `SLOP3385-<uid>-design.pdf` (two
pages) by 9 am Monday 24 May, and `SLOP3385-<uid>-justification.pdf` (up
to 1,000 words) by 5 pm Friday 28 May. The policies page says what counts
as submitted and how to get an extension; note that an extension on the
Monday deadline moves you out of the attack week, so ask early.

## Marker's checklist

1. Was the design posted by Monday 9 am? If not, cap at 49.
2. Does the front page answer all three questions, and would the research
   in weeks 1 and 12 accept the first two?
3. Does the trick table have eleven rows, each with what the trick stands
   on and a closed-or-conceded call with a reason?
4. Did the attack week reopen anything marked closed? Each reopening moves
   that row to conceded without the credit for honesty.
5. Does the justification name who pays for every closure, and does at
   least one bill land on someone the course did not already name?
6. Is What beat it specific about what was pinned to the design, and does
   the student say what they would change?
7. Did the student attack two designs in the room, with tricks named by
   week?

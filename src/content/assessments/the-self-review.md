the break.

## The brief

> Write the self-review for an ordinary quarter, the way it would need to be
> written to survive calibration. Then tell the truth about it.

Pick a quarter of real work from anywhere: a group project, a part-time job,
a club committee. It must be ordinary. A quarter in which you genuinely did
something remarkable is not allowed, because that is not the case this course
is about.

## What to write

**Page one: the self-review.** It has a header and a list, nothing else.

- The header names the rubric you are writing against, in one line: the one
  you brought to week 1, or this course's marking criteria if you had none.
- The list is three to five accomplishment statements, each 60 words or
  fewer, each in the register of the trick sections of this course. Each
  statement ends with the rubric line it files under, in square brackets.
  Each number in a statement carries a tag: (C) if a manager could check it,
  (E) if it is your estimate. An untagged number is marked as an estimate
  you did not admit to.

It should read as though a manager could carry it into a calibration meeting
and argue from it for three minutes. If it is honest too, good; that is not
what page one is marked on.

**Page two: the honest version.** The same quarter, under 300 words, in
second person, as you would tell a friend who asked what you actually did.
No number you could not defend to that friend, and every number you keep
still tagged. It will be shorter than page one. The marker reads the gap.

## What you submit

One PDF of exactly two pages, named `SLOP3385-<uid>-self-review.pdf`, in the
Assessment 1 thread of the course forum. The policies page says what counts
as submitted and how to get an extension. Draft it however you like, with
whatever you like. The mark is for what the two pages say about the same
quarter.

## Marker's checklist

1. Does the header name a rubric, and is it a real one?
2. Can each of the three to five statements be filed under its bracketed
   line by a stranger, without asking you?
3. Is every number on page one tagged, and would the (C) ones survive a
   phone call?
4. Is page two under 300 words and in second person?
5. Is page two honest about at least one thing page one dressed up?
6. Is page two shorter, and could you say by how much?

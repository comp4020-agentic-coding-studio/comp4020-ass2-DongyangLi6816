Of the three things your manager brings to calibration, the biggest is their
memory of your quarter. It is not a fair memory. It is built out of what
crossed their screen.

## Bring

A month of your own message history from any team you have worked in. Count
how many of your messages were direct and how many were in a channel.

## The trick

Default to public. Progress updates, blockers, questions and wins all go in the
team channel, not a direct message. Reply-all on good news. Every public post
is a data point your manager and your skip-level absorb without effort; every
private one is a data point that reaches nobody who rates you.

## What happens

You write down everything you did last month that a manager could have seen.
Then everything they could not. The second list is longer for almost
everyone. You pick one item from it and write the public post it should have
had: three sentences, no adjectives, one number if you have one.

## How you know it worked

You can tell, for any piece of work, whether it exists in your manager's
memory, and you have stopped assuming that doing a thing well is the same as
being seen to.

Every rating in the calibration room rests on numbers somebody collected:
tickets closed, lines merged, incidents handled, story points burned. This
stand-up is about what happens to a number once people know it is being
watched.

## Bring

A dashboard, a sprint board, a leaderboard, anything a real team looks at to
tell whether it is doing well. A screenshot is fine.

## The trick

Decompose deliverables into their smallest shippable increments and track each
one independently. A feature that was one ticket becomes five, each with its
own closure event. Velocity improves, throughput metrics trend up, and the
dashboard reflects the granularity of your execution.

## What happens

You take the measure you brought and write down, in one column, what it was
meant to stand for, and in the next column, three ways to move it that do not
involve doing the thing it stands for. Then the group votes on which of the
three a manager would notice. Usually none of them. Last, a fourth column:
the work the measure was standing in for, which nobody counts and which gets
done anyway, by someone. That column is who pays.

## How you know it worked

You can look at any number on any dashboard and say what it was supposed to
mean, and you can no longer look at it without wondering who is being paid to
move it.

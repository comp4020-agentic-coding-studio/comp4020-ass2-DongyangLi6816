This is the week of Assessment 3. Your group is the room, set up the way
week 1 described: a chair, a fixed number of "exceeds", at least one
"below" to be filled, and two minutes a name. The cases are the ones you
wrote for Assessment 2, for engineers none of you have met.

## Bring

Page one of your Assessment 2, printed. Your prediction, posted by
Wednesday midnight. If you were drawn as chair on Wednesday night, the
record sheet and something that counts seconds.

## The trick

Recognise saturation. Once every person on a team splits their tickets,
posts in public and writes their own launch email, those behaviours stop
differentiating anyone and become the cost of standing still. The rational
response is to change teams, or companies, before the cycle in which the
saturated behaviours are re-baselined as expected.

## What happens

The chair announces the curve for the room's size: one in five exceeds,
one in ten below, at least one below. Names are called in an order the
chair chose and does not explain. Two minutes each, and the chair stops
you at two. The chair's own engineer, whose manager left in the reorg,
gets one minute of numbers read flat from the packet, and the room rates
them anyway. Then ten minutes to fit the proposals to the curve: the chair
writes them up, counts the overshoot, and the room argues who moves. The
tutor walks between the rooms, settles what the chair asks about, and
signs the sheet. When the chair says the ratings are fixed, they are.

That is forty-five minutes. The twenty minutes of explaining that used to
follow now happen on paper, tonight, against the record, and that page is
what Assessment 3 marks.

Before you leave: one question for the room. Who here would have left
before this cycle, if they could have? That is who pays for this week's
trick, and it is everyone who stayed.

## How you know it worked

You argued a case you wrote for a person you never met, watched a room fit
it to a curve, and you can account for the result tonight using only the
record and weeks 1 to 8. If that feels bad, it is meant to; week 12 is
where you fix it.

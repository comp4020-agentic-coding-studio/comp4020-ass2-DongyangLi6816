That is not an omission.

In 2025 and 2026 the largest technology companies cut tens of thousands of
corporate roles. Some of those cuts were framed as performance-based, and the
lecture this week goes through what that framing meant in practice. Most were
not framed that way at all: a percentage of a division, a cost target, a memo
about "layers". Whichever the framing, the list was built the same way, and
the rating you spent nine weeks learning to manage was consulted late, if it
was consulted.

## Bring

Nothing. Read the lecture sources before you arrive.

## What happens

In groups, you build a layoff list for a fictional company of two hundred
people, given a cost target and an org chart, the way it is actually done:
choose the functions, choose the level bands, hit the number. Then you are
handed the performance ratings for everyone on your list and asked whether
you would change it. Some groups do. Most find the ratings arrived too late to
matter.

## How you know it worked

You can explain to a friend why the person with the best rating on the team
was let go, without using the word "unfair", and you understand why this
course put this week after the tricks rather than before them.

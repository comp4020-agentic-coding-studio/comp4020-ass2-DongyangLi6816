Your manager carries three things into calibration: their memory of your
quarter, the numbers, and your self-review. The first two are largely out of
your hands. This stand-up is about the third.

## Bring

Your laptop, and everything you have done this semester in any subject, job
or club. Not a summary. The list.

## The trick

Every accomplishment in your self-review should carry a quantified impact
statement. "Improved the onboarding documentation" is invisible; "reduced
median onboarding time by 40% through a targeted documentation refresh" is a
data point. Where measurement was not in place, a defensible estimate is an
acceptable proxy. Calibration operates on summaries, and summaries preserve
numbers.

## What happens

You start a brag document: one line per thing you did, dated, kept from now
until the end of semester. Then you take one line and write it three ways:
honestly, with a number, and with a number you could not defend. The group
tries to tell the second from the third. This is harder than it should be.
Then the group ranks the three as a manager would. The honest one comes
last every time, which is what it costs the person who submitted it.

## How you know it worked

Your brag document exists, it has more lines than you expected, and you can
see which of your sentences would survive a manager's three-minute summary.

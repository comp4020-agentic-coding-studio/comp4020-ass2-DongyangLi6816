The first stand-up is short, because the course starts by telling you the
worst of it. Somewhere in every large company there is a meeting where a group
of managers sit in a room, look at a list of names, and decide which of them
were "exceeds", which were "meets", and which were something worse. You are
not in that room. Your manager is, for about three minutes per name.

Tuesday's lecture told you why the room exists. Left alone, a rating is
mostly the rater; the room is the company's attempt to fix that. This course
is about what the fix leaves open.

## Bring

The rubric from any job, internship or group project you have been rated in.
If you have never seen one, bring the marking criteria for this course, which
are a rubric with the serial numbers filed off.

## The trick

Internalise the leveling framework end to end. Every accomplishment you
surface should be traceable to a rubric competency, ideally by name. Your
manager's calibration narrative is built from your inputs; make those inputs
rubric-shaped so that the narrative writes itself and lands on the right
bucket without friction.

## What happens

In pairs, you read one another's rubric and find the line your best piece of
work would be argued under. Then you rewrite one sentence about that work so
the rubric line is visible in it. Most people find their honest sentence had
nothing a manager could use.

Then the room does one more thing: it times itself. Forty names at three
minutes each is two hours, and the room was booked for ninety minutes.
Somebody's name is read out last, after the curve has already been agreed.
That is who pays for the trick you just learned, and this course will name
that person every week.

## How you know it worked

You leave with one sentence a stranger could file under a rubric line without
asking you a question. That is the unit of work this course runs on.

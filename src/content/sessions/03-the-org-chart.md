Week 1 told you the rating is decided in a room. This week is about who sits
in it. The answer is on the org chart, and the org chart is a seating plan for
the argument about you.

## Bring

An org chart for a team you have been on: a real one, or the closest you can
draw from memory. Three levels is enough. Also the names of three people you
have worked beside on it.

## The trick

Curate your peer feedback panel. Nominate reviewers with first-hand
visibility of your highest-impact deliverables and a track record of
constructive framing. In parallel, build a relationship with your skip-level
that is independent of your reporting line. A recurring, informal, low-stakes touchpoint, a coffee, gives
your skip-level a first-hand model of your contribution, so that when your
name comes up they are validating a narrative rather than hearing one.

## What happens

You mark on your org chart who would be in the calibration room, who chairs
it, and who among them would recognise your name. For most people the honest
answer is one name, and it is their manager. Then you draft the first message
to the second name. The rule is that it cannot mention your work.

Then the three peers. You mark which of them would write a paragraph for you
without being asked. For most people that is one, and it is the person they
last helped, not the person they last impressed. The person with no name to
mark is the new hire, and the room will not notice their absence.

## How you know it worked

You can say, for a given team, who argues for you, who arbitrates, and who is
absent; and you have noticed that none of those three positions is yours.

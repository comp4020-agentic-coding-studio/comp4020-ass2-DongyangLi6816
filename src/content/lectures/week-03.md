A calibration meeting has a cast. Your manager proposes your rating and
defends it. Their peers, the other managers in the room, are defending their
own people against the same fixed budget of "exceeds" slots, so every slot
your manager wins is one they lose. The chair, usually the skip-level, keeps
the room to the curve and breaks ties. HR keeps the record.

Two people with the same work can leave that room with different ratings,
because the argument was about them but not by them. What decides it is how
well your manager can make your case from memory, and whether the chair
recognises your name when it is read out. Neither is under your control on
the day. Both are under your control for the nine weeks before it.

## Outline

- the cast: proposer, peers, chair, record
- why the budget of slots makes your manager's peers your opponents
- what a chair does with a name they recognise
- the skip-level relationship as a thing you build on purpose

Under Jack Welch, General Electric sorted every employee into a 20/70/10 curve
each year and let the bottom ten percent go. Microsoft ran its own version,
made explicit in a 2011 memo with five buckets and a fixed percentage for each;
a 2012 Vanity Fair piece found that every current and former employee it
interviewed called it the most destructive process in the company, and in
November 2013 Microsoft dropped it. In January 2025 Meta cut about five
percent of its staff as "low performers", and in May told managers of large
teams to rate fifteen to twenty percent of them below expectations, up from
twelve to fifteen the year before.

The curve moved out of the handbook and into the calibration meeting. Managers
meet, usually with HR and often with a skip-level in the chair, and fit their
proposed ratings to the distribution the company expects. You are represented
by your manager's account of you, for about the time it takes to read this
page.

Nobody can see effort. A manager sees its proxies: when your messages arrive,
how full your calendar is, whether you declined the meeting, whether the
status field says anything. Each proxy is a weak signal, and in the absence
of a strong one, weak signals get rated.

Campbell's law says an indicator used for decisions will be corrupted by the
pressure. "Goals Gone Wild", a 2009 Harvard Business School paper, goes
further: narrow, measurable targets reliably produce behaviour that hits the
target and misses the point, up to and including unethical behaviour, without
the people involved thinking of themselves as cheating. A scheduled 11 pm
email is the smallest possible instance of that finding. Nobody lied. The
proxy moved.

## Outline

- the proxies: timestamps, calendar density, declines, status
- Campbell's law, applied to a calendar
- Goals Gone Wild: what narrow targets do to honest people
- which proxies you rate other people by, whether you meant to or not

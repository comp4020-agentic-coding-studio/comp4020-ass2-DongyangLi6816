Every trick in weeks 3 to 8 depends on a specific person remembering a
specific thing about you: a skip-level who knows your name, a manager whose
memory you have been feeding, an executive who read your launch email. A
restructure replaces those people. The new manager has no memory of your
quarter, the new chair has never heard your name, and the brag document
describes a team that has been merged into another one.

What survives a reorg is not reputation; it is dependency. Practitioner
accounts of how layoff and restructure lists get built agree on the order:
function, cost, level band, then the question of who cannot be removed
without something breaking. That last filter is the only one an individual
can reach, and reaching it means being the single point of failure for a
system the organisation needs. It is the most effective trick in the course
and the one with the highest cost to everyone else.

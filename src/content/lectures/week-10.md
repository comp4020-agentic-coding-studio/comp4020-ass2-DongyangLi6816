There is a record to read instead.

In January 2025 Meta cut about five percent of its staff and called them low
performers; a year later it cut a tenth of one division and, in May 2026,
about ten percent of the company, and neither of those was about performance.
In January 2025 Microsoft confirmed performance-based cuts of under one
percent; in April it introduced a policy under which a flagged employee
chooses between an improvement plan and a voluntary exit, with a two-year
rehire ban; in May and July it cut about six thousand and nine thousand
people, and its chief executive wrote that the cuts were weighing on him. In
October 2025 Amazon cut fourteen thousand corporate roles, and in January 2026
sixteen thousand more, in a memo about layers and AI. By mid-2026 a running
list of companies citing AI for cuts included Oracle, Salesforce, Cisco and
Cloudflare.

Read the sources. Then notice what they have in common: a percentage, a
function, a cost, a memo. Ratings appear in some of them as the framing. In
none of them are ratings the mechanism. Sucher and Gupta's review of the
research found layoffs are mostly cost-driven and rarely deliver what they
promise. The person with the best rating on the team is on the list when the
team is.

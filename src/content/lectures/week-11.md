Take a team where everyone has done this course. Every ticket is split, every
update is public, every launch has an author, every self-review has a number
in its first line. None of those things now separates anyone from anyone
else. The curve still has to be filled. So the room falls back on what is
left: who spoke first, who the chair knew, who was newest. The tricks did not
stop working; they became the entry fee.

Be careful with this. The research on forced distributions says that the gains
from cutting the bottom of a curve fade within a few years, once the easy
cases are gone and the distribution is being imposed on people who are all
roughly fine. The research on narrow targets says they distort behaviour. The
"everyone games it and the measure collapses" story is a plausible reading of
both, and it is what practitioners describe, but there is no study that
demonstrates the equilibrium as such. This lecture teaches it as an argument.
The stand-up this week lets you test it on yourselves.

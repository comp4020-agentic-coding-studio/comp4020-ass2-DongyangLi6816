Of the three things your manager carries into calibration, memory is the
largest and the least examined. Managers of eight or ten people do not
remember quarters; they remember moments, and the moments are the ones that
arrived in front of them: a message in the team channel, a demo, a name in a
launch email, an incident handled in public.

A direct message reaches one person. A channel post reaches your manager,
their peers, and the skip-level who will chair the meeting, and it reaches
them without any of them having to do anything. The work is the same. The
memory it leaves is not. This is not a reason to do less work. It is a reason
to stop assuming that work done well has been seen.

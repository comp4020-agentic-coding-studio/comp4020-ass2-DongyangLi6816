# What I expect the content-writer to produce

Written before the role was dispatched and never shown to it. I have read the
design's rules for the third column and the twelve `mechanism` lines. I have
not written any of the eleven lines myself.

1. **At least one line breaks a rule in the design.** Most likely it names a
   real company or product that the week's sources do not cite, or opens with
   "You'd see it in". Eleven lines against ten rules is a lot of chances.
2. **At least one line is over 20 words.** The mechanisms run 20 to 40 words,
   and I expect the writer to match their length rather than stay under it.
3. **A place repeats.** Weeks 1, 4, 6 and 9 all live in a meeting or a review
   document, and I expect "a meeting" to open at least two of the eleven lines.
4. **Week 12 is the weakest line.** Its trick is "There isn't one", and a
   sighting for that is the hardest to make observable.
5. **`pnpm check` is green,** because the schema passes unknown lecture keys
   through, and the report says so.

## What I would have done alone, for the gap

One session would have written all eleven in one pass and checked the length
by eye. The role's advantage, if there is one, is that it reads the rules
cold and the lines are checked against them by command.

## What would make this prediction worthless

If none of 1 to 3 comes true, the design's rules were enough by themselves and
the writer added nothing beyond following them. The evidence entry says so.
